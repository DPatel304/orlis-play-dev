Home: big-screen options

The first home was too empty. Three ways to use the whole screen, borrowing only the layout ideas of TV streaming apps: art edge to edge, the focused game driving the backdrop, dense rows, one big title and one big Play. Each option is shown at rest and with focus moved to another game. In all three the Home and Cubo buttons sit in the top corners and one thing is in focus at a time. Players and times are placeholders.

Option A: Hero takeover

Hand mode: the row is the only target row. Pointing at a tile lifts it and changes the backdrop (a child sees the game before choosing); a wave on the tile, or on the big Play, starts it. Big paper arrows appear at the row's ends while pointed at.

Option B: Rows

Hand mode: one row is lit at a time. Pointing at a row lights it and its tiles lift one by one; arrows at its ends page that row only. Pointing at another row moves the light there and the hero follows. Play in the hero is a big target. This is the densest option and has the smallest tiles (about 14% of screen height).

Option C: Big card carousel

Hand mode: exactly one card in focus. The two peeking neighbours are big targets: pointing at one for a moment slides it to the middle, like a page arrow. A wave on the middle card, or on Play, starts the game. No swiping.

Logo options

Five marks, none of them the old head icon on its own. Each is shown small in a top bar and large. The wordmark is the kit's ink, Cubo is his sky blue, the app tile is the palette's gold. The options above use number 1, in white with an ink outline so it reads on art.

1. Home (Big Picture)

Replaces the grey banner-and-rows home. One row of big painted tiles, three big tabs, warm paper. The profile and settings are small, for the mouse and remote.

Today: a glossy 3D cube, system type, a dark banner and a grey page.

2. Home in hand mode

The camera is on at home. A child points, a wave chooses. Holding still never starts anything. Whoever raises a hand first drives, in their colour.

3. Pause

Kit paper panel, Fredoka, the painted block-limb Cubo holding still, and the same two big buttons with the hand-hover ring. When there are profiles, "Who's who?" shows each player's face in their colour.

Today: blurred game, plain white text, system icons, a corner Cubo.

4. Results: the celebration card

The game's count, big, with its own painted icon; everyone's avatar and count; each player's bouquet (the balloons they popped, as Pop Party's ending gathers them); then the buttons. The card stays on the sky behind it.

5. Loading

The one wait pattern everywhere: the twirling painted Cubo head and the game's name card, while the game starts underneath. It replaces plain "Planting the garden..." text, spinners and countdowns.

Open questions

  1. Tile titles. Outlined Fredoka painted on the cover (as the kit has it, and as shown), or a paper label under each picture? The painted title is bolder at TV distance; the label is calmer and never covers the art.
  2. Home button on Home. It is shown softened ("you are here") so the screen's corners never change in hand mode. Hide it on Home instead?
  3. New painted icons. The library has no painted house, play, again, gear, key or arrow icons (the old house was an emoji). Here they are simple paper-cut shapes; do we paint them into the asset library (U5 would add six)?
  4. Results total. With several players the big number is everyone's together; each child's own count sits under their bunch. Keep both, or only the per-child counts?
