Orlis UI review

The play screen, home, tiles, icons, HUD, loading and transitions, judged against the vision: one painted, papery look for kids at TV distance, few words, and the same look on phones and in 3D. Screens from the current build at 1600 by 900.

What I recommend, in order

  1. Fix the four small bugs now (blurb contrast, ghost label, "1 points", the first-load flash). An afternoon for Sonnet.
  2. Design one Orlis UI kit before any new screen is built: paper background and cards, one HUD piece style, Fredoka, palette roles, painted icons, motion rules. The phone join page, the TV join card and Peekaboo’s HUD are about to be made; they should be born in the kit, not restyled later.
  3. Move the HUD onto the kit (all games get it at once, through the shared HUD code), and replace emoji with painted icons.
  4. Restyle home, pause and results onto the kit in the look milestone (M4), with a celebration results card.

Not reviewed: Discover, Create, Settings and sign-in as they look in the Windows app (the browser build shows placeholders there). Screenshots from the app, or a review in the Windows build, would cover them.

Fix now Home

The hero blurb is unreadable

Now: White text on the light fade under the banner. At TV distance it disappears completely.

Change: Put the blurb on the dark part of the banner, or give the fade a darker band; check every hero against a contrast rule (4.5:1).

Fix now Home

A ghost label under the tile title

Now: "Cubion’s Long Way Home" shows a faint second caption behind the painted title.

Change: Hide the plain caption once the painted title has drawn (keep it for screen readers only).

Fix now Results

"You got 1 points"

Now: Pop Party has no unit set, so it falls back to "points", and the plural is wrong.

Change: Give every game a unit ("balloons") and use one/many forms: "You popped 1 balloon", "12 balloons".

Fix now First load

The first seconds look unfinished

Now: Tiles fly in as placeholder gradients with glyphs and a blurred hero, then swap to the painted covers.

Change: Hold the row until covers are decoded (they are small), or show paper-coloured cards with the twirling Cubo head instead of placeholder art.

One look Everywhere

Two visual languages

Now: The games are warm, painted and papery; the menus are cool grey (#f2f4f7) with system type and glassy pills, closer to Apple TV than to Pop Party.

Change: One Orlis UI kit in the house style: warm paper background, cards with the paper edge and one soft shadow tone, Fredoka for titles and HUD (it is already bundled), palette roles shared with the games.

One look Icons

Three Cubos

Now: The top-right logo is a glossy 3D gradient cube; in games Cubo is flat, painted and outlined; the launch uses a third head.

Change: Use the painted Cubo head everywhere (logo, loading, profile), drawn by the asset library.

One look Icons

Emoji in the game screen

Now: Hint bubbles use emoji (balloon, leaves, bricks). They look different on every device and break the one-look rule.

Change: Draw hint icons from the asset library (it already has balloon, leaves, block, star); ban emoji in the UI and in Create’s hints.

One look Tiles

One tile is not painted

Now: The Discover tile is a gradient with a sparkle, next to painted key art.

Change: Paint it like a cover (Cubo with a parcel, say) so the Featured row reads as one set.

HUD HUD

Four styles of HUD piece

Now: The clock is a white pill; scores are blue pills with a thick dark outline; the move card is a heavy navy box; the goal bar and hints are soft white.

Change: One HUD piece style: paper white, a thin ink outline, one soft shadow; the player’s colour as a fill swatch, not a border. The move card becomes a small paper card with Cubo.

HUD HUD

The move card is too heavy

Now: A thick dark frame and stark white box, bigger than the clock, sitting on the grass for the whole opening.

Change: Smaller, paper style, and it tucks away after the first move is done.

Screens Results

The end is quiet

Now: "Well played! You got 1 points" in small type, bottom left; the avatar is big but nothing celebrates the number.

Change: A celebration card: the count big with the game’s painted icon (a balloon × 12), each player’s avatar with their count, the bouquet, then the two buttons.

Screens Pause

Pause works, could be warmer

Now: Clear and big. Blur and system type again feel like a different app.

Change: Same kit: a paper panel, Fredoka, the painted Cubo; keep the two big buttons and the hand-hover to choose.

Motion Launch

The launch flows well

Now: Tile grows into the cover, the name card appears at once, the Cubo head twirls, the game starts under it.

Change: Keep it. Make it the pattern: every wait in Orlis uses the twirling head (the 3D test says "Planting the garden…" in plain text).
