# Orlis Paper: the style rulebook (draft for the owner)

The look in one line: **cozy, playful, handmade paper**, for every kind of game (decision 0035). Visual board with six worlds:
`docs/style-board.html` (also a private page).

1. **Everything is paper.** Every thing is a flat paper cutout. Worlds are stacked paper layers in 2D; in 3D the same cutouts stand in a
   paper world, with folded-paper shapes (boxes and slopes wearing painted pictures) for ground and buildings.
2. **Layers cast a soft shadow** down and right onto the layer behind. This is what makes it read as paper.
3. **A thin white edge** on every cutout, about 1% of its height (owner's choice).
4. **Ink means "touch me".** Only things a player can touch, catch or pop get a dark ink outline (about 3% of their size). Scenery never.
5. **Flat colour, one shade, matte.** One colour per shape plus one darker tone on the shaded side. No shine, no gradients on things;
   gradients only in skies.
6. **Chunky and rounded.** Big simple shapes, soft corners, readable as a silhouette when small. Details only where they help.
7. **One face, one figure.** Everyone is a cube-headed paper figure with the same face style (dot eyes, simple mouth); animals and
   creatures share the face style.
8. **World palettes.** Each world takes a sky and five colours from one master palette. The brightest colours are kept for touchables.
9. **Paper motion.** Turn with a flip; appear by popping up from the ground; fall with a flutter; change screens with a page turn.
10. **Soft light, gentle haze.** Light from the top left; far layers fade toward the sky colour, more or less per world.

Open: the master palette itself (to extract from the current games and the board), the exact shadow offset and blur, and how rule 4
works in 3D (an outline shader or a drawn edge on standees).
