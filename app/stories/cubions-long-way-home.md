# Cubion's Long Way Home

The first Storytime story. About three minutes, nine pages, funny, plain prose. The refrain is
**"Cubes don't do that. But Cubo does."** Scenes use the games' painted look (the same sky, hills and flat colour as
Pop Party); the animals are the Animal Gym emoji; Cubo is his flat self. The first story is fixed (no kids' names in it).
Grown-up jokes stay (e.g. "Cubes land squarely").

Cues in square brackets are for the player: sounds, Cubo's faces, and the four gentle "join in" moments (the move card shows,
Cubo does the move, anyone can copy, nothing is checked).

---

**Page 1: the Cube Planet.** [scene: the Cube Planet, square houses and hills, the Elders in a row; sound: clicks]
Far, far away there is a planet where everything is square. The houses are square. The hills are square. Even the clouds sit in
neat little stacks. Nothing ever moves, because moving is messy. *Click, click,* said the tall, stern Cube Elders. That means,
"Please stay exactly where you are."

**Page 2: Cubion.** [scene: the Cube Planet; Cubo small among the cubes, one corner tilted; sound: clicks, then a rising CLICK]
One small cube was different. His name was Cubion!#&-551. (Say it fast, with lots of bleeps.) On Earth, everyone would call him
Cubo. Cubo wondered what it would feel like to move. The Elders did not like wondering. *Click-click-CLICK.* That means, "Go to
Earth, where everything wiggles, and learn how."

**Page 3: the landing.** [scene: the meadow; Cubo drops in; sound: thud; Cubo: wow]
Cubo landed on Earth with a great big THUD. Cubes don't land softly. Cubes land squarely. He looked around. Everything here was
moving! Leaves wiggled. Clouds drifted. And a very tall giraffe was stretching up to the sky.

**Page 4: the giraffe.** [Cubo: tongue out, then joy; sound: thud; MOVE: reach]
"Reach up high!" said the giraffe. Cubo reached with all his might. He was exactly as tall as before. He reached harder and tipped
right over. THUD. Then he looked up. He was a teeny bit taller. A hair taller. Cubes don't do that. But Cubo does.

**Page 5: the bunny.** [Cubo: grin; sound: flop, flop, a little boing; MOVE: jump]
Next came a bunny. "Hop!" said the bunny. Cubo hopped. Flop. Cubo hopped again. Flop. Then, for one wonderful second, he was off
the ground. By the width of a daisy. Cubes don't do that. But Cubo does.

**Page 6: the bird.** [Cubo: wow; sound: flaps, then a rustle; MOVE: flap]
A little bird fluttered by. "Flap!" chirped the bird. But Cubo has no wings, so he flapped his corners. Flap, flap, flap. Nothing.
Flap, flap, FLAP! He rose up a whole inch, and landed in a bush. Cubes don't do that. But Cubo does.

**Page 7: the frog.** [Cubo: smirk; sound: a squelch; MOVE: squat]
Last came a frog. "Squat down low," said the frog. Cubo squatted down low. He was very, very good at this. He was so good that he
couldn't get back up. "Cubes do that," said the frog. "And Cubo does it VERY well."

**Page 8: everyone wobbles.** [Cubo: joy; sounds: a trip, a splash, a crash, a flop, then a laugh]
Then something wonderful happened. The giraffe tripped over her own feet. The bunny landed in a pond. The bird missed her branch.
The frog flopped on his back. They were all wobbling! Cubo laughed his very first laugh. "Everybody wobbles," said the giraffe.
"That's the fun of it."

**Page 9: the long way home.** [scene: dusk; a warm window with a TV and children moving; a faint click; Cubo: smile]
Far away, the Elders clicked, asking if he'd learned the secret. Cubo thought about it. The secret was wobbling, and wobbling is
better with friends. Then he saw a warm glow in a window. A TV! With children in front of it, wobbling and laughing. It was a very
long way home. Just not the way he'd planned. So Cubo stayed. Cubes don't do that. But Cubo does.

*(The story just ends, then the end screen: "The end", Cubo waving, "Read again" and "Home".)*

---

Notes
- The refrain lands four times (giraffe, bunny, bird, and the last page), flips once (the frog), and closes the story.
- The name joke is used once (page 2) and not again.
- The Elders' clicks are recorded sounds; the narrator translates them.
- About 420 words: a slow read should come out near three minutes.
